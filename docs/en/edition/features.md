# Edition Features & Typography

The digital edition of Macdonell's *A Vedic Grammar for Students* is built upon the foundational rule of **strict 1:1 page fidelity**, combined with modern digital vector typography.

---

## 📐 The Principle of 1:1 Page Fidelity

In classical philology and Indology, exact page and section references are essential. A major shortcoming of earlier digital conversions (such as reflowed text dumps or differently paginated LaTeX transcriptions) is the loss of citation compatibility.

| Feature | Historical Print (Oxford 1916) | Digital Typeset Edition (2026) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Pages** | 520 pages | 520 pages |
| **Front Matter** | pp. `i`–`xii` (12 pages) | pp. `i`–`xii` (PDF labels `i`–`xii`) |
| **Main Text & Syntax** | pp. `1`–`380` | pp. `1`–`380` |
| **Appendices I–III** | pp. `381`–`470` | pp. `381`–`470` |
| **Indexes (Two-column)** | pp. `471`–`508` | pp. `471`–`508` |
| **Page Boundaries** | Original layout | Strictly congruent |

Any citation such as `Macdonell (1916), § 86, p. 78` points directly to page 78 in the digital edition without requiring any offset conversion.

## 🖋️ Clarendon Typography

The historic typesetting craft of the Clarendon Press was characterized by exceptional discipline:

### 1. Tabular Dot Leaders in Contents
The table of contents (pp. `ix`–`xii`) employs a rigid grid of tabular leader dots that align vertically and horizontally, guiding the eye cleanly to right-justified page numbers.

### 2. Column Headlines & Paragraph Counters
- Verso (even) pages carry the page number at the outer left margin followed by the opening paragraph bracket `[15`.
- Recto (odd) pages carry the closing paragraph bracket `15]` preceding the page number at the outer right margin.
- The center running head displays the subject matter in typographic small capitals.

### 3. Small Capitals
True small capitals are used throughout for headings, grammatical voices (*ACTIVE*, *MIDDLE*, *PASSIVE*), and proper nouns to maintain an even typographic color across each page.

### 4. Two-Column Register Typesetting
The concluding indexes are set in a two-column layout with center rules, indentation, and alphabetical section headers:

- **Vedic Index** (pp. 471–498)
- **General Index** (pp. 499–508)

## 🔤 Phonetic & Diacritic Precision

Vedic Sanskrit features archaic phonological stages that demand meticulous typography:

### IAST Standard
All Sanskrit vowels and consonants conform strictly to the International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration:

- **Sonant liquids**: `ṛ`, `ṝ`, `ḷ`, `ḹ`
- **Retroflexes**: `ṭ`, `ṭh`, `ḍ`, `ḍh`, `ṇ`, `ṣ`
- **Palatals & Velars**: `c`, `ch`, `j`, `jh`, `ñ`, `ś`, `ṅ`
- **Nasals & Breathings**: `ṃ` (Anusvāra), `ḥ` (Visarga)

### Vedic Accentuation System
Unlike Classical Sanskrit, Vedic was a musical pitch-accent language. This edition reproduces all accent notations:

- **Udātta** (high pitch): Represented by acute or high vertical stroke.
- **Anudātta** (low pitch): Represented by a horizontal underline beneath the syllable.
- **Svarita** (falling pitch glide): Represented by circumflex or upper vertical accent.

## 🧭 Navigation & PDF Architecture

The PDF file is optimized for scholarly workflows in modern PDF readers (Acrobat, Preview, PDF Viewer, Zotero):

- **Hierarchical Bookmark Outline**: 81 chapters, sections, and indexes are directly navigatable in the PDF sidebar.
- **Native Page Labels**:
    - `i` through `xii` for Front Matter.
    - `1` through `508` for the main body (entering `150` in the reader jumps directly to book page 150).
- **Vector Rendering**: Infinite scalability, sharp rendering on high-DPI Retina screens, and clean print reproduction.
